= Results
:description: Plot groups and plots, datasets, derived values, tables and export.

Everything under *Results* works on the solutions of your studies. You can add, change and re-plot results without solving again.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Node |Purpose
|*Datasets* |What results are computed on: a study's solution, or a cut line, cut plane, grid, revolution, mirror and so on, made from one.
|*Derived Values* |Numbers: integrals, averages, maxima and minima, point values, global quantities.
|*Tables* |The numbers that derived values produce.
|*Plot groups* |A 3D, 2D, 1D or polar view holding one or more plots.
|*Export* |Images, data, VTU files and reports.
|===

== Plot groups

Add one with menu:Results[3D Plot Group], *2D Plot Group*, *1D Plot Group* or *Polar Plot Group*. Each plot group has:

* a _Dataset_ and a _Solution selection_: the last solution, a time, a parameter value or an eigenmode;
* a _Title_ (automatic or your own), and a color legend with optional max/min;
* for 1D groups, axis labels, log scales, *Manual axis limits* (x and y minimum and maximum, in the plotted units), grid and legend position.

Plots inside the group inherit its dataset (_Dataset: From parent_) unless they set their own. Click *Plot* (or press kbd:[F8]) to redraw. A study creates default plot groups when it finishes, for example *Temperature (ht)* and *Isothermal Contours (ht)*.

=== Plots for 3D and 2D groups

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Plot |Shows
|*Surface* |An expression on the surfaces (3D) or over the domains (2D), with a color table, or uniform color and wireframe.
|*Volume* |An expression inside the domains (3D).
|*Slice*, *Multislice* |Planes through the model, set by count or coordinates. Multislice uses several planes in x, y and z at once.
|*Isosurface* |Surfaces of constant value, with a level legend.
|*Contour* |Lines of constant value (2D) as lines, tubes or filled bands, optionally with level labels.
|*Arrow Volume* |Arrows of a vector field, at the mesh nodes or on a regular grid, with normalized or scaled length.
|*Streamline* |Streamlines of a vector field, started on selected boundaries or at points, as lines, tubes or ribbons.
|*Mesh* |The mesh of the dataset.
|*Line* |An expression along edges or boundary curves, as lines or tubes.
|*Annotation* |Text at a position, with `+eval(expr, unit)+` for values.
|*Radiation Pattern* |An acoustic exterior-field quantity on a circle (polar) or sphere (3D).
|===

Attributes you can add under a plot:

* *Deformation*: shows the plot on the deformed shape, for example the displacement in solid mechanics, scaled.
* *Selection*: restricts the plot to some domains or boundaries.
* *Color Expression*: colors a plot (arrows, streamlines, lines) by another expression.

The color range is the minimum and maximum of the plotted values, unless you set _Manual color range_.
A field that is constant to within 1e-9 of its value is drawn in a single color. Examples are a uniform current density and a fixed temperature. Its nodal values differ only by round-off, and stretching the color table over that tiny range would show the noise as a gradient.
The legend's ▲ and ▼ markers then show the value.

[#click-evaluate]
=== Click a plot to get its value

Click a point of a 3D or 2D result plot in the Graphics window to read the plotted value there, as in COMSOL. Each click adds a row to the *Evaluation 2D* or *Evaluation 3D* table and a line to *Messages*. The table appears under *Tables* and in the *Table* pane. A row holds:

* the clicked coordinates in the geometry length unit: x, y (and z), or r, z on 2D axisymmetric models;
* the value of every plot in the group that uses the clicked plot's dataset, one column per expression, in each plot's unit. For example, a Surface of `+normB+` and a Contour of `+Az+` give a T column and a Wb/m column.

The value is computed by the code behind *Point Evaluation*, so a Point Evaluation at the same coordinates gives the same number. Some details:

* Surface, Volume, Slice, Multislice, Isosurface, Contour and Line plots report their expression. Arrow and Streamline plots report their *Color Expression*, or the norm of the vector field if they have none. Mesh plots and dataset edges are not picked; a click goes through them to the plot beneath. In 3D a click lands on the front-most plotted surface.
* On a deformed plot, the row gives the material (undeformed) coordinates of the point you clicked and the value there. This matches a Point Evaluation at those coordinates.
* A plot with its own dataset reports from that dataset. Plots on other datasets in the same group are left out of that click's row.
* A click off the plotted geometry adds nothing.
* When a click reports an expression or unit the table does not have yet, it adds a column; the earlier rows are left empty there.
* Clicks in 1D and polar plot groups do not evaluate: these graphs are drawn by matplotlib, not in the 3D/2D Graphics view. For values at a point, use a *Point Evaluation* or a *Cut Point* with a *Point Graph*.

Delete the *Evaluation 2D* or *Evaluation 3D* table node to start a new table; the next click creates it again.

=== Plots for 1D and polar groups

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Plot |Shows
|*Line Graph* |An expression along a Cut Line (or along boundaries in 2D, including a Box Selection), against arc length or a coordinate. With *All* in the plot group's selection, one curve per output time, and per parameter value of a Parametric Sweep.
|*Point Graph* |An expression at a Cut Point against time or the sweep parameter.
|*Global* |Global quantities (coupling operators such as `+aveop1(T)+`, eigenfrequencies, electrode currents) against time, frequency or the sweep parameter.
|*Table Graph* |Columns of a Table.
|*Line Graph (Polar)* |An expression along a circle, against angle.
|*Directivity* |The exterior-field level against angle and frequency.
|===

Each graph has line style, width, color, markers and a legend.

== Expressions and units

Each plot has an expression and a unit. Click the button beside the expression for the list of variables in the solution. Expressions combine:

* solution variables with COMSOL-style names (`+T+`, `+ht.tfluxMag+`, `+solid.mises+`, `+spf.U+`, `+cd.iloc+`, `+acpr.Lp+`, …), listed in xref:reference:result-variables.adoc[Result variables];
* coordinates `+x+`, `+y+`, `+z+`, parameters, Definitions variables and coupling operators;
* functions and operators, including `+real+`, `+imag+`, `+abs+`, `+arg+`, `+conj+` for complex fields.

The _Unit_ list offers the units of the expression's quantity (°C, °F or K for a temperature, MPa for a stress, mm for a displacement, …).

Parameters in result expressions take the values the solution was computed with. If you change a parameter after computing, the existing solution keeps the old value, and *Messages* says which parameters differ from the model until you compute again. Parameters added after the solve are available for postprocessing.

== Datasets

With several components, every component has its own *Solution* dataset per study, and a plot or evaluation sees the component of its dataset. See xref:models-and-files.adoc#components[Several components]. A plot group, plot or evaluation whose study is out of date gets the asterisk mark too, with _Shows Study 1, which is out of date_ in its tooltip.


[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Dataset |Gives
|*Solution* |A study's solution (created with the study).
|*Cut Line* |Points along a line from Point 1 to Point 2, for line graphs.
|*Cut Point* |One point, optionally snapped to the closest boundary, for point graphs.
|*Cut Plane* |A plane through a 3D solution, for a 2D plot group (quick plane at a coordinate, or point and normal).
|*Parametric Extrusion 2D* |A 2D dataset or cut plane extruded over the solutions of a sweep, the parameter becoming the third axis.
|*Revolution 2D* |A 2D axisymmetric solution revolved into 3D, over a start and revolution angle, with layers.
|*Mirror* |A dataset mirrored in a plane (3D) or line (2D). Datasets can be chained, for example a mirrored cut plane.
|*Grid 2D*, *Grid 3D* |A regular grid of points, which may extend beyond the mesh (acoustic exterior fields).
|*Parametric Curve 3D* |A curve x(s), y(s), z(s), for example a spiral around a loudspeaker.
|===

== Derived values

Add them from menu:Results[...] (Evaluation group) or by right-clicking *Derived Values*. Give one or more expressions with units and descriptions, then click *Evaluate*. The result goes to the *Table* pane and a *Table* node.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Derived value |Computes
|*Volume/Surface/Line Integration* |The integral of an expression over the selected domains or boundaries. For a boundary flux such as `+cd.iloc+`, the integral equals the conservative total current.
|*Volume Average* |The integral divided by the volume.
|*Volume Maximum/Minimum* |The extreme value and its location.
|*Point Evaluation* |Values at clicked geometry points or at coordinates.
|*Global Evaluation* |Global expressions (coupling operators, parameters, eigenfrequencies, electrode currents), for every solution of a sweep at once if you choose.
|===

The names follow the space dimension, as in COMSOL: in 2D, *Surface Integration* integrates over domains and *Line Integration* over boundaries.

=== 2D axisymmetric models

In 2D axisymmetric models, integrals and averages are taken in the *revolved geometry*, weighted by 2πr (_Compute integral in revolved geometry_, on by default). A *Surface Integration* of a heat flux over a boundary is then the total heat flow through the revolved surface. Clear the check box to integrate over the r-z plane.

== Tables

Each evaluation writes to a Table (shown in the *Table* pane). Clicks on 2D and 3D plots collect in *Evaluation 2D* and *Evaluation 3D* (see <<click-evaluate>>). Copy it to the clipboard or export it as CSV with the pane's buttons, or plot it with a *Table Graph*.

[#export]
== Export

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Node |Writes
|*Image* |A PNG of a plot group, at a given width and height.
|*Animation* |A plot group at every output time (frequency, eigenmode, parameter value) of its dataset, or at a given _Number of frames_ spread evenly over them, as an animated GIF (frames per second, repeat) or as numbered PNG images. The plot group's own solution selection is restored afterwards. A dataset with a single solution has nothing to animate and is refused.
|*Data* |The node coordinates and comma-separated expressions, as CSV.
|*VTK Unstructured Grid* |The solution as `.vtu`, for ParaView.
|*Report* |An HTML report with the parameters, the model tree and images of the plot groups.
|===

Click *Export* at the top of the node's Settings window to write the file. *Image Snapshot* in the Graphics toolbar saves the current view directly.

Every results node and setting is listed in the reference: xref:reference:plots.adoc[Plots], xref:reference:datasets.adoc[Datasets], xref:reference:derived-values.adoc[Derived values], xref:reference:export.adoc[Export].
